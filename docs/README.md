# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

Welcome to the documentation hub for OctoAcme's project management processes. This README serves as the entry point for contributors and team members, providing a high-level summary of how OctoAcme runs projects and quick links to all process documentation.

## Overview

OctoAcme uses an iterative, artifact-driven project management approach centered on customer value, clear ownership, and continuous improvement. Every project follows a defined lifecycle—initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective—supported by standard templates for charters, backlogs, risk registers, and release notes. Decisions are data-informed, delivery is incremental, and psychological safety is encouraged so that feedback flows freely across the team.

Roles are clearly defined to ensure accountability at every stage. The **Project Manager (PM)** coordinates schedules, risks, and communications; the **Product Manager (PdM)** owns the product vision and prioritizes the backlog; **Developers** implement and test features; **QA/Testing** validates quality; and **Stakeholders** provide inputs and approvals. These personas work together through a regular cadence of weekly PM–PdM syncs, twice-weekly standups, and monthly stakeholder updates, with ad-hoc escalations following a clear path from team level to PM to Product Lead to Sponsor.

Risk management and stakeholder communication are treated as ongoing activities, not one-time events. Risks are identified during planning and tracked throughout execution in a shared risk register, with owners and mitigation plans. Communication follows structured templates—weekly status updates, incident reports, and escalation paths—ensuring a single source of truth for project status at all times.

Quality and learning are built into the process through rigorous acceptance criteria, a Definition of Done, and structured retrospectives after each sprint, release, or milestone. Teams capture what went well and what to improve, convert findings into backlog action items, and measure the impact of those changes—creating a culture of small, iterative improvements that compounds over time.

## How to Use These Docs

- **New to OctoAcme?** Start with the [Project Management Overview](octoacme-project-management-overview.md) and [Roles and Personas](octoacme-roles-and-personas.md) to understand the team structure and principles.
- **Starting a new project?** Follow the [Project Initiation Guide](octoacme-project-initiation.md) and then move to [Project Planning](octoacme-project-planning.md).
- **During delivery?** Refer to [Execution & Tracking](octoacme-execution-and-tracking.md) and [Risk Management & Communication](octoacme-risks-and-communication.md).
- **Releasing?** See the [Release & Deployment Guide](octoacme-release-and-deployment.md).
- **After the project?** Run a [Retrospective & Continuous Improvement](octoacme-retrospective-and-continuous-improvement.md) session to capture learnings.
